Signal — Training Stack Results

orbitope · MARL ladder over Signal.EnvServer · sep 2026 · all numbers measured, greedy eval, same harness

One compact multi-agent PPO trains every rung of the ladder over the existing traffic sim. Capacity is never the constraint — the largest brain here is 62k parameters. What the runs actually establish: a shared local policy with a locally-computable pressure reward beats fixed-time control everywhere, generalizes across topologies, and — the result worth the trip — recovers the coordination a centralized critic would buy, precisely when the network saturates.

Independent (one brain per intersection, neighbours blinded), shared (one brain, all intersections), and CTDE (shared actor + centralized critic). Light, symmetric demand.

All three RL rungs beat fixed-time. Shared ≈ independent, and both beat the heavier centralized critic — because grid2 at light demand is low-coupling, so seeing your neighbours buys almost nothing. That is a clue, not a disappointment; it sets up the corridor.

A real network — the corridor (15 intersections)

Not a uniform grid: a fast two-way primary arterial (E–W thoroughfare) crossing a secondary arterial, a one-way eastbound side street (the reverse links simply don't exist; the router honours it), and demand concentrated on the thoroughfares. Built by CorridorBuilder.cs, rendered from the true authored network:

The shared policy masters it — beating fixed-time on the pressure objective and on average wait under both stationary demand (−148 vs −184) and time-varying rush-hour demand (−117 vs −144), tracking a load that ramps and drains.

The result — coordination pays under saturation

At light demand, seeing your neighbours is worth almost nothing. Retrain both the shared (neighbour-aware) and independent (blind) policies at a saturating 1.8× demand — where queues actually spill back between intersections — and the gap opens:

Across 3 seeds each, evaluated at 1.8× (the starved% column is the fraction of approach waits pinned at the 120 s clamp — a fairness/starvation proxy that, unlike max-wait, doesn't saturate to a constant):

The 24-point return gap is ~5× the seed spread — a robust effect. And the blind independent policy nearly collapses to fixed-time's return: with no neighbour signal it cannot beat local greedy once the network is coupled. Coordination's value is demand-dependent: negligible at light load, decisive under saturation — the plan's thesis, measured.

Scale — one brain, 1 to 25 intersections

The shared policy is agent-count-agnostic (a fixed 105-float view per intersection; N never enters the network), so the identical 62k-param brain runs every size. Trained fresh on grid5 (25 intersections) it beats fixed-time comfortably:

And a brain trained on grid2 (4) runs grid3 (9) zero-shot, beating that grid's own fixed baseline (−165 vs −214). The only cost of scale is env throughput — the single-threaded sim: ~8,800 decision-steps/s at 1 intersection, ~1,200 at 15, ~860 at 25. The learner and model are untouched.

Honest caveats & the next rung

Transfer holds within a lane structure; crossing structures (single-lane grid ↔ bays with protected-left phases) needs the curriculum ladder the plan prescribes — a brain that never saw a bay never learned its protected-left phases. Even under saturation the 1-hop coordination win is real but not a landslide (~10%), which is the honest motivation for richer attention/GNN policies. Reward-vs-metric tension persists on dominant-flow corridors (a dumb "hold the arterial green" posts low wait but worst total pressure). Next: retrain the coordination comparison on the 25-grid under load to see whether the neighbour advantage widens with network size, and export trajectories to watch the green wave the shared policy builds.
